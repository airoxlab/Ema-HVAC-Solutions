Common questions

Good to know

My AC stopped working in the middle of a heat wave — what now?

Call (323) 608-1020 and describe what it's doing: no air at all, warm air, tripping the breaker, or a noise it wasn't making before. That usually narrows the fault before we arrive. While you wait, check the thermostat batteries and whether the breaker has tripped — and if the outdoor unit is running but the air is warm, switch the system off so you don't damage the compressor.

Why does my AC run all day and never cool the house?

In Valley summers a system that never catches up is usually one of a few things: low refrigerant from a leak, a dirty condenser coil that can't dump heat, a failing capacitor, or ductwork losing cooled air into the attic. It can also mean the unit is undersized for the house. We'll measure rather than guess, and tell you which it is.

Is it worth repairing my AC or replacing it?

It depends on the age of the system, what failed, and the cost of the repair against a replacement. A ten-year-old unit with a bad capacitor is worth fixing. A twenty-year-old system needing a compressor usually isn't. We'll tell you honestly which situation you're in, including when the cheaper repair is the right call.

How often should an AC system be serviced?

Once a year, ideally in spring before the first heat wave. Valley systems run hard for months at a time, and most mid-summer breakdowns start as something a tune-up would have caught — a weak capacitor, low refrigerant charge, or a condenser coil packed with dust.
